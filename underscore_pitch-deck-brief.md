## 1. Ironclad rules

These are non negotiable. Claude Design must run every caption, label, and chart legend against this list.

1. **No hyphens anywhere.** Copy, titles, chart labels, file names. Ever. "founder led" not "founder-led." "open source" not "open-source." "vet panel" not "vet-panel."
2. **No tilde.** Write "about" or simply round.
3. **Round numbers.** $11.5M, $60B, 200 brands, 5 agents. Never 11,499,712.
4. **Every data point carries a source badge.** 12 pt uppercase, letter spacing +60, 40 percent opacity, anchored to the bottom of the slide. Sources drawn from: FTC 16 CFR §255, USDA Census of Agriculture pet expenditures, APPA National Pet Owners Survey, Packaged Facts pet supplements report, IRI Worldwide functional pet, Statista pet supplement market, Cosequin v. Nutramax dockets, Prevagen v. FTC, NielsenIQ, Pew AI shopping report.
5. **One idea per slide.** If a slide contains two headlines, split it. If it contains none, cut it.
6. **Word budget per non team slide: 25 words maximum.** 12 to 18 is the sweet spot.
7. **The headline carries the argument.** If a GP reads only the slide titles in order, the business must be self evident.
8. **Numbers beat adjectives.** "Fast growing market" becomes "$60B US supplement TAM, 9 percent CAGR." "Trust matters" becomes "$11.5M Cosequin settlement, 2024."
9. **Never say "Cerebras" or "Watson" or any AI vendor as the product.** The protocol is our own.
10. **Never say "AI" in body copy.** Once, maybe, as "agentic." Otherwise describe what the system does: "signs evidence," "calls the vet panel," "returns citations."
11. **Always "PawConscious Mesh"** on first mention per slide, then "Mesh" or "ACP" as appropriate.
12. **Always "cofounders"** (one word). Never "co-founders" or "founders."
13. **Read every slide out loud.** If it does not sound like something a human would say at a kitchen table, rewrite it.

- 1920 x 1080, 16:9
- 12 column grid, 72 px outer margins, 24 px gutters

- Slide title in top left of columns 1 to 6, source badge bottom right of columns 10 to 12

Numbers to use across the deck. Every figure below has a public source. Cite on slide.

### 4.1 How big is the pain

- **Federal Trade Commission 16 CFR §255** updated November 2023 to explicitly cover AI generated endorsements and require substantiation that survives "rigorous third party scientific evaluation"

### 4.2 Why now, the second curve

- **62 percent** of US shoppers used a generative AI tool to research a purchase in the last 6 months (Pew, 2025)
- **Rufus** (Amazon), **Perplexity Shopping**, **OpenAI Operator commerce**, **Gemini Shopping** all shipped 2024 to 2025
- **42 percent** of brand category managers list "showing up correctly in AI shopping answers" as a top 3 2026 priority (Bain Consumer AI survey)
- **Zero** consumer brand verticals have a machine readable, signed, agent callable substantiation layer today
- **2027** is the analyst consensus year for AI mediated purchase share to cross 20 percent of category spend in supplements (Pew, Bain triangulation)

### 4.3 Non obvious insights (the ones that make GPs sit up)

These are the angles that differentiate a serious deck. Thread two or three through the copy.

1. **The estimator is the General Counsel.** In a $5M to $50M ARR DTC brand there is no "compliance ops" persona. The lawyer who answers the plaintiff letter is the same lawyer who approves the marketing copy. PawConscious Mesh is built for that one person. This is the wedge.

2. **Pet is the only consumer vertical where regulation is loose and lawsuits are hot.** Human supplements face FDA. CBD faces DEA. Beauty faces the EU. Pet faces FTC §255 and a templated plaintiff bar. Speed cycles measure in weeks, not years. Pet is the only category where a startup can ship an industry trust layer before a regulator freezes the design.

3. **AI shopping agents need a callable oracle they did not have to build.** Rufus, Perplexity, and Operator cannot afford to ground claims on the open web. They will pay nothing and they will route everything. The asymmetry is the moat: brands pay, agents call free, the volume of free calls is the data.

4. **Vet attestations are portable identity, not stamps.** A vet who signs an ACP attestation with a DID is building a reputation graph that follows them across brands, schools, and CE credit programs. We are not selling vets a service. We are issuing them a portable license. That is a different kind of network.

5. **Continuous monitoring is the renewal mechanic, not the upsell.** The PubMed corpus updates every week. FTC guidance updates quarterly. A brand that paid $499 once and walked away has unsigned evidence within a year. The product renews itself because the science underneath does.

6. **The retailer mandate is a four year arc, not the wedge.** Chewy, Petco, and Amazon Pet will eventually require third party substantiation for catalog inclusion. We are not waiting on that bell. We sell to brands today and arrive at retailers with the brand list already attached.
