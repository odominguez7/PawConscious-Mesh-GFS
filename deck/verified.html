What it means when a PawConscious product is verified

A PawConscious-verified product carries a signed evidence bundle that any AI agent, retailer, or human reader can independently check. This page explains exactly what is being verified, who does the verifying, and how the process holds up against the FTC, plaintiffs, and the next agentic shopper.

1 What "verified" means

Every claim on the label is mapped to evidence, scored by a vet panel, and cryptographically signed

A PawConscious-verified product page links to a PCEC v0.1 evidence bundle. PCEC stands for Pet Claim Evidence Certificate. The bundle is a structured JSON document, signed with Ed25519, that anchors every health claim on the product label to its underlying evidence.

This is not a static badge or a PNG seal. It is a machine-readable document that an Amazon Rufus, a Chewy product agent, or a plaintiff's law firm can fetch, parse, and audit in seconds.

2 How the verification runs

Four gates, every claim, every time

When a brand submits a product URL to PawConscious, the system parses every health claim on the label and runs each one through four independent gates. A claim only earns SIGNED status if it passes all four. A bundle is published the moment the last gate clears.

Gate 1
Claim extraction
The product page is fetched and every health claim on the label is isolated and normalized to a canonical form. The same claim said two ways becomes one canonical claim.
Gate 2
Citation search
Each claim is matched against the indexed corpus through BioMCP (PubMed), AI2 Asta (peer-reviewed life-sciences corpus), and the FDA animal-drug-approval registry. Only sources with full citation chains qualify.
Gate 3
Independent vet review
A vet on the stipend-funded panel reviews the claim against its citations and assigns a vet_score from 1 to 5. Below 3 the claim is rejected and the bundle is not signed.
Gate 4
FTC §255.3 compliance
The claim and its evidence are checked against the FTC's Endorsement Guides §255.3 standard for substantiation. Claims that fail are flagged, surfaced to the brand, and excluded from the signed bundle.

Each gate is logged with a timestamp and an operator identifier, and the full audit trail is appended to a Firestore transparency log that anyone can read.

3 Who does the verifying

An independent vet panel, paid by PawConscious, not by the brand

Independence is the load-bearing claim of this protocol. PawConscious pays the vet panel a flat stipend per claim reviewed. Brands cannot pay the reviewer directly, cannot pick the reviewer, and cannot see the reviewer's identity during the review window.

Reviewer
Stipend-funded vet panel
Licensed veterinarians sourced from the Tufts, Cornell, and University of Pennsylvania veterinary schools, each contracted by PawConscious on a per-claim stipend basis, with conflict disclosures on file.
Citation source
PubMed · AI2 Asta · FDA registry
Open scientific literature accessed via the BioMCP and Asta MCP servers. No proprietary or brand-supplied corpus is admitted.
Regulator check
FTC §255.3 substantiation rubric
Every claim is graded against the FTC Endorsement Guides §255.3 standard, the same rule the Cosequin and VetriScience plaintiffs used to win their settlements.
Cryptographic signer
PawConscious certificate authority
The bundle is signed with an Ed25519 key managed by PawConscious. The public key is posted on the PawConscious site and on the Linux Foundation A2A registry for any party to verify.
4 What a verified bundle unlocks

The same document plays four roles for four audiences

A signed PCEC bundle is not just a stamp. It is the single artifact that lets an AI agent recommend the product, lets the brand defend the claim in court, lets the retailer surface it on their platform, and lets the pet owner read a plain-language summary on the product page.

For AI agents
A callable trust source
Amazon Rufus, Chewy's recommender, or any A2A v0.3 agent can fetch the bundle, verify the signature, and surface the product as evidence-backed to the shopper.
For brands
A plaintiff defense in 90 seconds
Every claim on the label is pre-mapped to citations, vet review, and FTC §255.3 evidence. When the plaintiff bar comes knocking, the brand publishes the bundle URL and the case dissolves.
For retailers
A catalog filter
Chewy, Petco, and Amazon Pet can filter their catalog to show only products with active PCEC bundles, raising trust scores and reducing platform liability.
For pet owners
Plain-language proof
The product page renders a human-readable summary of every claim, what it is backed by, and who reviewed it, with no PNG badge or opaque seal between the buyer and the evidence.
