# 16 CFR § 255.0 - Purpose and Definitions

## (a) Purpose
The Guides interpret FTC Act Section 5 regarding endorsements and testimonials in advertising. They provide "the basis for voluntary compliance with the law by advertisers and endorsers." Practices conflicting with these Guides may trigger corrective FTC action if the Commission determines they violate the statute. The Guides outline general evaluation principles with illustrative examples, though they don't address every possible endorsement scenario.

## (b) Endorsement Definition
An endorsement is "any advertising, marketing, or promotional message for a product that consumers are likely to believe reflects the opinions, beliefs, findings, or experiences of a party other than the sponsoring advertiser." Forms include verbal statements, social media tags, demonstrations, and use of names, signatures, or likenesses.

## (c) Endorsements and Testimonials
The Commission treats endorsements and testimonials identically for enforcement purposes.

## (d) Product Definition
"Product" encompasses any product, service, brand, company, or industry.

## (e) Expert Definition
An expert possesses "knowledge of a particular subject, which knowledge is superior to what ordinary individuals generally acquire."

## (f) Clear and Conspicuous
This means disclosures are "difficult to miss (i.e., easily noticeable) and easily understandable by ordinary consumers," appearing in relevant communication modes and remaining unavoidable and uncontradicted.

Source: 16 CFR Part 255, FTC Endorsement Guides (2023 update). Federal regulation, public domain.
