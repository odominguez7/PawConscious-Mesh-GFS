# 16 CFR § 255.5 - Disclosure of Material Connections

(a) Core Requirement: When an endorser has a connection to a product seller that could materially affect credibility, and audiences wouldn't reasonably expect it, disclosure is required. The regulation defines material connections broadly: "Material connections can include a business, family, or personal relationship" as well as monetary payments, free products, early access, or other benefits.

The disclosure must "clearly communicate the nature of the connection sufficiently for consumers to evaluate its significance," though complete details aren't necessary.

(b) Fourteen Illustrative Examples cover scenarios such as:

- Funded research: Drug companies must disclose when they fund studies cited in advertisements
- Celebrity compensation: Standard commercial payments typically don't require disclosure, but unusual arrangements (equity stakes, ongoing royalties) do
- Medical endorsements: Healthcare influencers must disclose paid spokesperson relationships
- Social media posts: Influencers receiving free products must disclose the arrangement
- Employee endorsements: Company employees promoting products online must reveal their employment relationship
- Incentivized reviews: Programs paying consumers for reviews require clear disclosure of compensation
- Affiliate links: Bloggers earning commissions from product links must disclose this arrangement

## Implications for pet brand endorsements

For pet supplements:
- A veterinarian on retainer with a brand must disclose the relationship.
- "Vet-recommended" or "vet-formulated" claims with material connections require disclosure.
- Influencers receiving free pet products must disclose the arrangement.
- Funded clinical studies on pet products cited in advertising must disclose the funding source.

Source: 16 CFR Part 255, FTC Endorsement Guides (2023 update). Federal regulation, public domain.
